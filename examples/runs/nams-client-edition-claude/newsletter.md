## The lead: Brain organoids pass five years and remember their own development
Harvard neuroscientists in Paola Arlotta's lab sustained human brain organoids for more than five years, three times the previous record of 694 days set in 2021 by a UCLA and Stanford team. The findings were published this week in Nature, per a Harvard Faculty of Arts and Sciences article dated Aug. 19, 2026. Each peppercorn-sized organoid held over 1 million human-donor cerebral cortex cells. The team profiled 34 organoids by single-cell RNA sequencing at eight timepoints between six months and five years; with prior work, the dataset spans 110 organoids and nearly 425,000 cells. Cells faithfully modeled the molecular developmental sequence of human brains during gestation and the first years of life, and DNA methylation used as an age clock replicated the same steps documented in human brains. Longevity came from a liquid medium designed to promote spontaneous electrical signaling plus an amino acid supplement as auxiliary energy: within nine months neurons increased and formed denser synapses, and after one year all organoids in the new medium showed vigorous bursts of electrical activity while none in the other did, activity documented for two years. When cells of different ages and donors were combined into one organoid, using the chimeroid technique lead authors Irene Faravelli and Noelia Anton-Bolanos published two years ago, younger progenitors made early-stage cells while older ones immediately made later-stage neurons normally produced two or three months later. The team concluded cells retain a memory of steps already performed; Arlotta, a little bit shocked, called it a time warp, and framed the work as a proof of principle, with the next task making development happen faster. NIH funded the research. ([Harvard Faculty of Arts and Sciences](https://current.fas.harvard.edu/stories/lab-grown-brain-organoids-set-longevity-record))

## Worth your time
- **[ProteinTalks: perturbation proteomics foundation model for virtual cells](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.07.637070v1)** — A bioRxiv preprint posted February 10, 2025 (10.1101/2025.02.07.637070) from Westlake University, with co-authors at DP Technology, Westlake Omics, the AI for Science Institute and Peking University, describes ProteinTalks, a neural ordinary differential equation foundation model. The authors report generating over 38 million perturbed protein measurements in breast cancer cell lines, on the premise that a virtual cell requires comprehensive understanding of protein network dynamics. Pretraining, the abstract says, gives a fundamental understanding of those dynamics; the model encodes protein networks and shows consistently improved predictive accuracy across downstream tasks. In cancer cells it robustly predicts drug efficacy and synergy, identifies novel drug combinations and, through interpretability, uncovers resistance-associated proteins; in patient-derived tumor xenografts it predicts potential drug responses, and with clinical data enhances breast cancer prognosis prediction. Corresponding authors Guo and Zhu are Westlake Omics shareholders. Preprint, not peer reviewed.
- **[SPOT: shape, appearance and motion phenotyping in 3D organoid imaging](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.28.691111v1)** — Posted to bioRxiv November 29, 2025 (10.1101/2025.11.28.691111), largely from Oxford's Ludwig Institute for Cancer Research, with first author Felix Y. Zhou and last author Xin Lu both corresponding alongside twelve co-authors, SPOT pairs a standardized Shape-Appearance-Motion phenome with a SAM-Phenotype-Observation-Tool, described respectively as an image-transcriptome and an image-transcriptome analyzer. The authors state the pair gives unbiased, comprehensive description of morpho-dynamic phenotypes without prior knowledge, and report applying SAM-SPOT to their own simulated organoid database with known ground truth and to more than 1.6 million mouse and human organoid instances under defined genetic and chemical perturbations. They report it can effectively and robustly characterize 3D morpho-dynamics from 2D projection videos, and that with single-cell RNA sequencing it revealed altered WNT signaling, but not mutant RAS or p53, predisposes intestinal organoids to irregular morphogenesis. A provisional patent is pending; not peer reviewed.
- **[Emulate submits full ISTAND qualification package for Human Liver-Chip](https://emulatebio.com/press/emulate-enters-final-stage-of-fda-istand-program/)** — Emulate announced on September 9, 2026 that it submitted its full FDA ISTAND qualification package for the Human Liver-Chip, describing this as the final stage of the process and as advancing its effort to establish Organ-on-a-Chip as a qualified Drug Development Tool for drug-induced liver injury assessment. Per the company, the package shows reproducibility across three independent laboratories using three different primary human hepatocyte donors; consistent ability to distinguish structurally similar compounds with different liver toxicity profiles; repeat studies months apart producing consistent results; and translation to the high-throughput AVA Emulation System, supporting up to 96 independent Organ-Chip samples per run. Initial qualification data came from the Zoe platform, and Emulate says this is the first time AVA data will be evaluated in FDA qualification. If FDA ultimately qualifies the chip, Emulate says, that would set an important regulatory precedent.
- **[CDER's table of streamlined nonclinical contexts and acceptable NAMs](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/center-drug-evaluation-and-research-cder/cder-streamlined-nonclinical-studies-and-acceptable-new-approach-methodologies-nams)** — CDER publishes a page, CDER Streamlined Nonclinical Studies and Acceptable New Approach Methodologies (NAMs), with a table inventorying drug development contexts where the center is open to a streamlined nonclinical program, including but not limited to reduced sample size, reduced number of species tested, and use of NAMs. Categories span Safety Pharmacology, General Toxicity, Carcinogenicity, DART, Juvenile Animal Toxicology, Special Toxicity, Screens and Other Opportunities. For anticancer pharmaceuticals where cause for concern warrants a QT prolongation assessment, hiPSC-CM studies following best practices can replace the animal study otherwise conducted for QT prolongation or proarrhythmic risk; safety pharmacology endpoints may also be incorporated into general toxicity studies, routine for biologics. The table excludes 505(b)(2), generic and biosimilar pathways plus impurities and excipients, provides general information only, and will be updated; sponsors are encouraged to discuss programs with review divisions, which will evaluate acceptability.
- **[EMA voluntary data submission pilot for NAM data](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/voluntary-data-submission-pilot-advance-innovative-alternatives-animal-testing)** — An EMA news item dated 1 September 2026 announces a voluntary data submission pilot for NAM data, intended to foster regulatory acceptance by letting regulators gain experience and assess scientific value in a regulatory context. NAMs here can include complex in vitro systems such as organoids and microphysiological systems, computational models, and combinations of methodologies. The structured, non-binding framework lets companies, method developers, CROs, academic laboratories and other stakeholders submit and discuss NAM data independently of a marketing authorisation application, with individual, non-binding and non-decisional feedback from an operational expert group of non-clinical experts from across the European medicines regulatory network. EMA says such data are rarely shared with regulators today, limiting confidence-building. Interested organisations submit an application form; after initial validation, selected applicants are invited to submit a full briefing package for scientific assessment. Submissions are expected until Q3 2027, then a lessons-learned report (EMA/149393/2026).
- **[Complement-ARIE Reduction to Practice Challenge: twenty Phase 1 winners](https://commonfund.nih.gov/complementarie/news/phase-1-winners-announced-complement-arie-reduction-practice-challenge-prize)** — The NIH Common Fund's Complement-ARIE program announced twenty Phase 1 winners of the Reduction to Practice Challenge Prize in a July 20, 2026 public webinar; all are eligible for Phase 2. Run with NASA's Tournament Lab, the prize invites integrated human-based NAM solutions demonstrable in practical, usable form within three years, catalyzing near-ready combinatorial platforms that can be submitted to the Complement-ARIE Validation and Qualification Network, managed by the Foundation for NIH. Phase 1 entries required a clear intended use and a feasible refinement plan; winners provided strong early data. Named winners include Predictive Brain Combinatorial NAM (Massachusetts General Hospital), Human Lung Models for Anti-Fibrotic Drug Testing and AI-Driven Antibody Repurposing (Wake Forest), CAR-T Immunotherapy Clinical Trials on a Chip, AI-integrated human skin immune organoids (Stanford), LIVER-COMBINE, Gut Feelings (Cedars-Sinai), and platforms from Qureator, VeriSIM Life, Javelin Biotech and Frontier Bio.
- **[FDA/HESI/SPS workshop on ICH S7A and NAMs in safety pharmacology](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fdahealth-and-environmental-sciences-institutesafety-pharmacology-society-hybrid-workshop-ich-s7a)** — FDA, with the Health and Environmental Sciences Institute and the Safety Pharmacology Society, scheduled a hybrid public workshop, ICH S7A and New Approach Methodologies (NAMs) in Safety Pharmacology, for July 28, 2026, 8:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. ET at the White Oak Campus and via Teams. FDA says the one-day meeting aims to unite regulatory and industry representatives around a cohesive vision and scientific rationale for revising ICH S7A, building consensus to reopen the guideline through exchange of real-world experience and case studies. Stated objectives: a shared cross-regional understanding of NAMs in safety pharmacology; clarified regulatory opportunities and constraints for NAM inclusion in S7A; discipline-specific use cases; and actionable recommendations. Citing a recent FDA roadmap emphasizing reduced animal testing, FDA states the event will not result in specific changes to ICH guidelines, though the information exchanged will shape future regulatory policy.
